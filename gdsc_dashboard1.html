Does gene expression predict how a cancer cell line responds to a drug?

A cancer cell line's response to a drug depends on its biology — which genes are switched on, which are mutated. This project tests how well that biology, captured as data, can predict drug sensitivity — across five cancer types, using real genomic and pharmacological datasets.

WHAT'S BEING PREDICTED
LN_IC50 — a standard measure of drug potency. Lower values mean the cell line is more sensitive to the drug; higher means more resistant.
WHAT R² MEANS HERE
The share of variance in observed drug sensitivity the model explains. 0.79 means the model accounts for ~79% of the variation in observed LN_IC50 — a strong result for biological data this noisy.
HOW IT WAS BUILT
XGBoost regression models, trained separately per cancer type, on features spanning drug identity, gene expression, mutation status, and clinical markers like MSI.

Per-cancer model comparison

Four gene-selection stages tested per cancer: all 40 original genes, a cancer-specific priority subset, priority + literature-flagged missing genes, and all 40 + missing genes with nothing removed.

What the pattern says

BREAST / LUNG

Both improved consistently as genes were added or curated — Breast reached 0.769 and Lung/NSCLC reached 0.790 with priority + missing genes. These cancers are driven by pathways well covered in the gene list: hormone receptor signaling, DNA repair, RTK signaling.

OVARIAN

Best with the priority subset alone (0.769) — smallest sample of the five (27 cell lines), so treat this result more cautiously than the others.

COLORECTAL

Adding more expression genes didn't help — but dropping expression entirely and using only BRAF/KRAS/NRAS mutation status beat every expression-based attempt, reaching 0.773 (vs 0.760 baseline) with just 6 features. Mutation carries more signal than expression here.

MELANOMA

Same story as Colorectal — expression alone plateaued around 0.747, but mutation status alone reached 0.766, the best result for this cancer in the whole project. Confirms mutation and expression capture different, non-additive information for these two cancers.

Colorectal & melanoma — focused follow-up

Both cancers plateaued on gene expression alone, so three different types of biological information were tested on top of the baseline, one at a time, to isolate what actually helps.

Where the signal actually comes from

Feature importance from the Colorectal and Melanoma mutation-test models — the textbook driver genes (BRAF, KRAS) show up strongly as mutation status, not as expression level. This is the core evidence behind the mutation-vs-expression finding.
